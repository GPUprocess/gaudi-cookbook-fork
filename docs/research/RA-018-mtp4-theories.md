### 0.3 The failure pattern (the key finding of this audit)

**Three structurally unrelated optimizations (graph-captured draft, async replay, mHC row cap) each flip exactly one suite item — R003 (bat-and-ball, expected "5" → answers "10") — deterministically.** This is not three bugs; it is one phenomenon: R003 is a **knife-edge item** whose answer sits on a bimodal/near-tie distribution, and ULP-level numerical perturbations move which mode wins. The corpus (GaudiGrimoire, tier D — abotsis `LAYER0_DELTA_FINDINGS` §10-12, the third GLM port's patch 51, and the abotsis fork's determinism doc) identifies the ULP sources precisely:

1. **HCCL all-reduce is not row-invariant** (abotsis §11, confirmed 2026-09-13): HCCL sums each chunk of the `[bs, hidden]` buffer in a chunk-dependent rank order; at decode a chunk is ONE decode row, so a row's residual after o_proj/MoE down-proj depends on its batch position by ~1 bf16 ULP. "GLM-5.3 carries the ULP in the KDA recurrent state of every layer after the first and MoE routing flips at near-ties turn it into different greedy tokens." Their fix: `VLLM_HPU_ALLREDUCE_MODE=gather_sum` (all_gather the partials, sum in fixed rank order in fp32) — 8 identical prompts: 7 distinct texts → 1 text; serial-vs-concurrent parity 7/12 → 12/12 (with pinned decode buckets); **no measurable decode cost (14.7 vs 13.6 tok/s, restart noise)**. The patch exists in the corpus and is portable (gaudi-llm-notes `patches/optional/51-hpu-allreduce-order-invariant.patch`, 70 lines, compile-safe, default `hccl` = inert; buffers >16 MiB fall back to hccl because captured-graph sites retain working buffers). **Our tree does not have it** (RESEARCH-001 addendum 2; B022 port candidate, never done).
2. **Batch-shape numerics (inherent)**: GEMM/attention kernels round differently at different shapes, exactly as on other accelerators. abotsis: with gather_sum alone, serial-vs-concurrent parity was 7/12 on the default bucket ladder; **pinning every decode to one recipe** (`VLLM_DECODE_BS_BUCKET_MIN/STEP/MAX` — already in our tree, untested) → 12/12 identical, warmup 13→2 min, no decode cost (padded rows are free; decode is host-launch-bound).
3. **Captured-region compilation (the Q01v2 source)**: with bit-identical attention inputs and weights, `attention_raw_output` differs by ~1 bf16 ULP across batch/compile contexts (relL2 3-4e-3), and the delta stays bounded (2-6e-2) through all 45 layers — "amplification via mHC mixing, no blow-up signature of a logic error." This is the mode-independent drift the implementation agent measured (whole = split = 1/12). abotsis exonerated everything else (tensor cache, mark_steps, lazy-acc-par, KDA state handling, MoE routing, fp8 activation scaling) — the residual is the compiled region's own fusion/reduction shapes.

**Implication:** the 0.5 pp accuracy gate (2 items of 40) is currently the BINDING WALL for this project's three biggest banked optimizations — and it is tripped by one knife-edge item, not by a real regression. The gate as configured cannot distinguish a 1-ULP distribution shift on a trap question from a genuine regression. This is theory T7 below.

## 1. The MTP=4 wall: what exists, what is missing (evidence-based)

DECISIONS.md roofline: **200 t/s bs=1 requires MTP-4 with acceptance ≥ 4.2 at 100% spec BW — the measured 3.33 is below it** (see T4 for the consequence).

Our tree's state (TASKS.md): A011 (k=2) functionally complete, performance-refuted under eager draft; **A012 (k≥3) NOT INVESTIGATED, "blocked on the draft-execution lever (A014) because eager per-step cost grows with depth"**; Q11 (k=2/k=4 revival under draft graphs) BLOCKED on Q01; RA-004 (MTP k=4 graphed chain) IN REVIEW, gated on RA-002.

## 2. The theories (multiple, ranked)

### T1 — The numerics-invariance package: port `gather_sum` + pin decode buckets + align the captured draft cores (the unlock for everything)

**Hypothesis:** the three rejected optimizations (+20% draft graph, +29% async, +19% MHC prefill) were rejected by the SAME single knife-edge item (R003), and the R003 flips are driven by the two fixable ULP sources (HCCL AR row order, bucket-ladder shape numerics) plus the captured-region drift. Remove the fixable sources, and the draft graph (W2) clears the gate; MTP=4 (W6) then has a realistic path.

**The package (three parts, in cost order):**
1. **Port `VLLM_HPU_ALLREDUCE_MODE=gather_sum`** (env + 70-line patch, corpus-sourced: gaudi-llm-notes patch 51, ported from abotsis pr/core commit 1587118; compile-safe; default `hccl` keeps the patch inert; the 16 MiB buffer cap falls back to hccl for co-batched prefill). Expected: identical rows become identical outputs (the row-position ULP source dies); no decode cost (their measured 14.7 vs 13.6 — restart noise).
2. **Pin the decode buckets** (`VLLM_DECODE_BS_BUCKET_MIN=32, STEP=32, MAX=32` at ns32, or the ns16 equivalent — the knobs are IN our tree, untested per RESEARCH-001 addendum 2 "free win for boot time and determinism"). Expected: one decode recipe → serial-vs-concurrent parity (their 7/12 → 12/12); warmup 13→2 min; decode cost ~0 (padded rows free; the Q14v2 first-batch capture cost is one-time).
3. **Align the captured draft cores with the eager draft** (the implementation agent's own next action for Q01v2: diff the compiled recipe of a captured core vs the eager equivalent, or force fp32 accumulators in `_DraftPreAttentionGraphCore`/`_DraftPostAttentionGraphCore` — the draft is a small module; the extra precision is free; the VERIFY target stays bf16). Expected: the captured-region drift (the mode-independent 1/12 source) shrinks; the golden gate recovers.

**Why it works (mechanism):** sources 1-2 are position/shape-invariance fixes — they make the output a function of the TOKENS, not of the row position or the bucket. Source 3 attacks the residual (the compiled region's own reduction shapes) — either by aligning them (recipe diff → pin the offending op) or by raising the accumulator precision (the ULP no longer lands on the bf16 boundary). The abotsis boundary diagnostics prove the residual is BOUNDED (2-6e-2 over 45 layers, "no blow-up") — it is not a logic error; it is rounding. Rounding at a 0.75-nat near-tie (R003's measured gap) flips; rounding at a 3-nat gap does not. The package does not (and cannot) make MTP bit-identical to non-MTP — it makes the flips predictable, attributable, and (for the gate) excusable (T7).

**Evidence quality:** the AR row-invariance root cause is PROVEN at boundary level in the corpus (abotsis §10-12: the layer-0 output differs deterministically by row index; the KDA state carries the ULP; 18 expert-routing flips follow; gather_sum + pinned buckets → 12/12). The transfer risk to our tree (different stack: v0.26.0/1.24.1 vs their v0.27.2rc0/1.24): LOW for the AR fix (the HCCL chunk-order behavior is vendor-level, stack-independent); MEDIUM for the bucket pinning (our bucketing is `VLLM_BUCKETING_STRATEGY=lin` + `VLLM_DECODE_BS_BUCKET_*` — same knobs); MEDIUM for source 3 (our captured region is ours; the recipe diff is the measurement that decides).

**Verification protocol (for the implementation agent):**
- Boot A (baseline): the adopted config, suite v1 (5 reps) + the 8-identical-prompt rowdep probe (the abotsis probe: 8 identical prompts decoded concurrently → count distinct texts; expect ~7 distinct today, the pre-fix signature).
- Boot B: + gather_sum (patch). Measure: rowdep (expect 1 distinct), suite, decode bs=1/@16 (expect parity), the >16 MiB fallback log (confirm no prefill path is silently hccl'd where it matters).
- Boot C: B + pinned decode buckets. Measure: serial-vs-concurrent golden (expect 12/12), warmup time, decode.
- Boot D: C + the Q01v2 draft graphs (split then whole). Measure: the golden gate (expect ≥ 10/12; target 12/12 or the residual flips all attributed to top-2 gaps < 1.0), suite (expect ≥ 99.5%), the +20% re-confirmed.
- **Gate:** if D passes the suite (or the flips are all attributed near-tie per T7's rule), the draft graph is ADOPTED → Q11's k=2/k=4 sweep starts (next action per TASKS.md).

**Cost:** 1-3 boots (B and C can share a boot family if the env is hot-swappable — it is not, the patch needs a redeploy; realistically 2-3 boots), days not weeks. No HPU-time risk beyond the boots.

**Risks:** (a) the gather_sum working buffers inside CAPTURED prefill graphs (the 16 MiB cap exists for exactly this; the decode [32, 4096] bf16 buffer × 8 ranks = 2 MiB — far below; prefill >16 MiB falls back — the prefill first-token parity degrades to today's behavior, a wash); (b) source 3 may not fully close the 1/12 (the compiled-region drift may be irreducible at bf16 — in which case T7's gate policy is the remaining unlock; the package still delivers the rowdep/serial-parity wins); (c) the pinned-bucket decode pads a lone request to the full bucket (ns32: a bs=1 request runs a 32-row recipe — abotsis measured this as free at their host-bound regime; our bs=1 is also host-bound (45 ms step, 24 ms host) so the prediction holds, but the A/B must confirm).

**This is the top-ranked theory because it is the only one that is (a) cheap, (b) evidence-backed at boundary level, (c) a precondition for W2/W6, and (d) simultaneously unlocks three other banked optimizations (the +20% draft graph, the +29% RA-011 follow-up, the +19% MHC prefill) — four wins per boot family.**

### T2 — The chain graph: capture the entire 4-step MTP draft chain as ONE HPU graph per decode bucket

**Hypothesis:** the per-draft-step seam (`mark_step` + full `torch.hpu.synchronize()` + `mark_step`) is the W3 wall: at k=4 it is 4 full device syncs per step, each serializing the host/device pipeline for that phase — the exact mechanism that killed the early-draft pipelining (EXP-Q19) and that caps how much of the draft's device time can hide. Capturing the WHOLE chain (4 draft steps: step j+1's inputs — the drafted id j and hidden state j — are INTERNAL graph dataflow through the captured argmax/eh_proj) as one graph per decode bucket reduces it to ONE replay + ONE seam per step.

**Why it should work (mechanism + evidence):**
- The chain's per-step dataflow (drafted token j → embedding → eh_proj → layer-45 MLA+MoE → shared head → argmax → token j+1) is a fixed op sequence with fixed shapes per bucket — capturable. The only per-step host-varying inputs are the attention metadata tensors (block tables, slot mappings for the draft MLA's KV writes at positions p+1..p+4, the draft positions) — the Q01 attn-mode core ALREADY takes the md tensors as positional graph inputs (`_DraftAttnGraphCore.forward(*md_tensors)`); the chain version takes 4× the md tensors (or the 4 per-step slot mappings, which are fixed offsets from the row's base position — capturable as constants per bucket).
- The abotsis reference runs `VLLM_GLM_MTP_DRAFT_ATTN=1` + `VLLM_GLM_MTP_ATTN_GRAPH=1` ("draft attention captured as one graph per step") — per-STEP, not per-chain; our chain graph is the one-level extension (vLLM's EAGLE-style propose graphs do exactly this on GPU: the whole multi-step propose in one graph). The in-graph AR inside the draft MoE is supported (lazy collectives in HPUGraph — G7; our verify graphs already contain the 162 ARs).
- The dry-run hazard is already handled in-tree (`dry_run=False` is load-bearing — the comment documents the upstream garbage measurement); the chain graph keeps the same setting.
- **The payoff math (ESTIMATED):** today k=4 whole-mode = 4×(replay + FULL SYNC + mark_step). The full sync costs the draft phase's device tail (the draft's own device work ~1-3 ms/step at k=4 — the MTP layer is small: 1 MLA + 1 MoE block) PLUS it destroys the overlap of the host post-work (sampler/reject walk) with the device — the Q19 mechanism. Chain graph: 1 replay + 1 sync → the draft marginal cost becomes ~device-only AND the early-draft pipelining (GLM_EARLY_DRAFT=1, adopted) can resume. The Q11 arithmetic flips: at k=2 the draft marginal drops from "2×(device+host+sync)" to "~2×device" — the REFUTED verdict ("revisit only under the in-graph draft") was written for exactly this change.

**Risks:** (a) the captured argmax + the 4-step dataflow may hit a lazy-capture limitation (the draft chain is a data-dependent loop UNROLLED at capture — it is a fixed 4-step sequence, no loop — so capture is legal; the drafted ids are TENSORS flowing through, not Python values — the vLLM GPU propose-graphs prove the pattern); (b) the draft MLA's KV writes (the drafted tokens' latent KV for the NEXT step's draft) must land in the right slots — the per-step slot mappings as graph inputs (the attn-mode core's existing pattern); (c) one chain graph per (bs bucket × block bucket) — the pinned-bucket recipe (T1.2) bounds the count; (d) capture-time cost: 4× the per-shape capture (the chain is 4× the ops) — one-time, minutes per shape.

**Verification:** CPU parity (chain graph output == the 4-step eager chain, the `check_draft_graph.py` contract-test pattern); the golden gate under T1's regime; the Q11 protocol (k=2 then k=4 sweep, parity suite per TASKS.md's "next action"); the early-draft A/B (chain graph + GLM_EARLY_DRAFT=1 vs chain graph alone — the pipelining restoration measurement).

**Dependency:** T1 first (the accuracy regime), then T2 (the performance unlock). T2 is the actual "make MTP=4 work" engine: W1+W3 die in one change.

### T3 — Fuse the KDA verify conv-chain (the spec_len=5 device micro-op storm)

**Hypothesis:** at k=4 the port's KDA verify conv chain (a Python `for j in range(spec_len)` loop of same-shape elementwise ops, per KDA layer, INSIDE the captured decode graph) adds ~1,190 small device ops/step (5 tokens × ~7 ops × 34 KDA layers) — each small op on HPU has ~1-5 µs of launch/queue overhead in the recipe replay → ~1-6 ms/step of device overhead at k=4 that is pure overhead (the math itself is trivial). A fused conv-chain kernel removes it.

**The fix options (in cost order):**
1. **Closed-form bmm (no kernel, pure torch, capture-safe):** the depthwise conv `out[b,j,c] = silu(Σ_t win_or_x[b,j+t,c] · w[c,t] + b[c])` over the chained window is LINEAR in the input — rewrite as one batched matmul: build the padded sequence `x_padded[b, j+W-1, c] = [win[b, 0..W-2, c], x_cat[b, 0..L-1, c]]` (one cat + one index_select — 2 ops), then `out_pre = x_padded @ w_cat` per channel-group (a [bs·L, C] × [C, W] bmm with the W-1 shifts gathered — or, since the weight is depthwise (w[c,t] per channel), a strided-slice sum over W shifts: W=4 → 3 shifted multiplies + adds + silu = ~10 ops total per layer instead of ~35). Capture-safe (no unfold, no broadcast forms — the same ops the loop uses, just hoisted out of the Python loop).
2. **A TPC kernel** (one kernel: the [bs, L, W, C] unfold-free conv chain + the state scatters in-register; the Q21/B7 infrastructure — the op lib loading is now proven — makes a custom kernel deployable; the vendor's GDN issue describes exactly this op family for the recurrent path).
3. **The vendor multi-token GDN decode** (the `hpu_fused_recurrent_gated_delta_rule` spec variant the community issue requests — if Intel ships it in a later SW release, it supersedes both our custom conv-chain and the recurrence path; track at the v0.30.0 upgrade question, companion doc).

**Expected (ESTIMATED):** −1 to −6 ms/step at k=4 (device), more at k>4; at k=1-2 the effect is smaller (the loop is 2-3 iterations). The recurrence itself (`hpu_recurrent_kda(..., return_all_states=True)`) is already a single call — no action.

**Risks:** LOW for option 1 (pure algebraic rewrite, the CPU contract test `check_kda_spec.py` pins the math against the stepped reference — the abotsis test pattern does exactly this, L=5 included: "L=2 is MTP-1, L=5 is the MTP-4 target"); the HPU kernel path (option 2) carries the usual kernel-qualification cost.

**Ranking note:** T3 is real but SECONDARY — it is a device-side trim inside an already-captured graph; the walls W1-W3 (host/sync/accuracy) dominate. Do it after T1/T2 land, in the same boot family as the k=4 measurement (it is part of the k=4 kernel hygiene).

### T6 — The async completion: RA-011 + async-D2H + candidate-slot pipelining (the host-side end-state)

**Hypothesis:** even with T1-T3, the k=4 step at bs=1 is: host (replay loop ~14 ms + sampler/reject ~2.5 ms) serialized with device (~7-12 ms with Q21: 0.86-3.5 ms weight floor + attention ~1.5 + dense/MTP + AR 3.4) → step ≈ 20-26 ms → 3.33/0.023 ≈ **145-165 t/s** (ESTIMATED). The RA-011 async replay (+29% MEASURED at k=1 — the host term off the critical path at bs=1) is the missing half, and its rejection was a FIXABLE output-path bug (the in-flight consumption of the cached outputs — my R3, now confirmed by the implementation agent's own analysis: "the overlapping host work consuming the graph's cached OUTPUT tensors while the device is still executing"). The vendor's own docs scope the flag to "along with stream and async D2H to copy outputs" — the redesign is: **before the host consumes step N's outputs, enqueue an async D2H copy of the consumed tensors (token ids, the reject-walk inputs, the logprobs) into a pinned staging buffer; the tensor-cache reuse of the graph's output slots becomes safe because the host reads the copy, not the slot.** With the KDA candidate slots (T2/W4), the state-resume semantics are already safe (the next step loads column num_accepted — the draft for step N+1 needs only the accepted prefix's state).

**The end-state physics with everything (all ESTIMATED, for the 200 bs=1 / 800@16 targets):**
- bs=1, k=4 (MTP head): Q21 (0.86-3.5 ms) + chain graph (T2: draft ~device-only) + async+D2H (T6: host ~5-8 ms off the critical path) → step ≈ max(host ~6-8, device ~7-12) ≈ **8-12 ms** → 3.33/0.010 ≈ **165-220 t/s** — the 200 t/s target is INSIDE the envelope at its top (the same "inside the envelope, four credible items" structure as the @16 model in BOTTLENECK_RESEARCH §7).
- bs=1, k=4 + block drafter (T4, acceptance ~4-5): → **200-280 t/s** — the target with margin.
- @16, k=4: the weight read is the full 38.1 GB (all 36 local experts touched at 80 verify rows — no routed-only saving at high batch, as today); the step's weight term is unchanged (15.5 ms at peak BW, ~22 ms at 69% measured utilization), the compute term grows with rows (80 vs 32), the tokens/step rise 1.85 → 3.33 → net @16 ≈ **300-450 t/s** (ESTIMATED; the abotsis reference measured 185.9 @16 on a base ~2.5-3× SLOWER than ours — consistent). Q21 at @16 does NOT cut the weight term (all experts touched) but cuts the launch count (the ~50 MoE ops → 42 calls) and the AR boundaries — the @16 gain is the acceptance × (1 − launch/AR overhead). **The 800@16 target at k=4 is NOT inside the envelope on the stock weight-read; it requires the Q21 kernel's launch/AR reduction PLUS (per the DECISIONS roofline) the acceptance — or the ECF8-fused weight floor cut (RA-017, −14.5% on the weight term) on top.**

**Risks:** the async-D2H redesign is a runner change (the output-read path — `hpu_rejection_sampler.py`'s `.cpu()` calls, the sampler's host consumption, the logprobs path) — MEDIUM complexity; the vendor's thread-pool branch (PT_HPU_ENABLE_HPUGRAPH_THREAD + PT_HPU_QUEUE_SYNLAUNCHES) is tier-B-verified (G1) and the +29% is measured — the mechanism risk is low, the integration risk is the D2H staging correctness (the watch items from RA-011's verification protocol: the hang/garbage signatures of RJ-18-class engine-async are NOT the failure mode here — RA-011 keeps the engine loop synchronous, only the replay is async).

**Dependency:** T1 (the accuracy regime — the async path must not re-introduce R003 flips; the in-flight fix is precisely what keeps the outputs stable) + Q21 (the device term must be small enough that the overlap pays at bs=1 — at the stock 15.5 ms weight floor the device already dominates and the async gain is the measured +29% either way).

### T7 — The gate is the wall: an MTP-aware, tie-aware accuracy gate (policy)

**Hypothesis:** the binding constraint on MTP=4 (and on three banked optimizations) is not performance or numerics — it is the GATE. The 0.5 pp bar (2 items of 40) auto-rejects any configuration that flips ONE knife-edge item, and R003 (bat-and-ball, a TRAP question where "10" is the wrong-but-plausible answer) sits exactly on a bimodal near-tie. Three independent optimizations flip it deterministically to "10" — the signature of a distribution-mode flip at a ~0.75-nat gap (R003's measured logprob gap under Q01v2: "10" at −0.392 vs "5" at −1.142), not of a regression. A regression that moved R003's gap from 3.0 to 0.5 would be a different signature (the top-2 gap SHRINKING); the observed flips are at gaps that are ALREADY small (all Q01v2 golden divergences at top-2 gaps 0.125-1.875).

**The proposed gate (for the owner's decision — the implementation agent already recorded the policy question after the third flip):**
1. **Keep the 0.5 pp bar for NON-knife-edge items** (the real-regression detector, unchanged).
2. **Knife-edge items are tie-aware:** a flip on an item is EXCUSED (counted separately, reported) when AND only when (a) the divergence position's top-2 gap under the TREATMENT config is < 1.0 nat (the measured near-tie class), AND (b) the item's expected answer is a trap/ambiguous construction (R003-class: the bat-and-ball "10" mode). The attribution is measured, not assumed: the I015 boundary diagnostics (the abotsis layer-diagnostic tooling, port READY per TASKS.md) or the logprob-diff tooling (the Q01b protocol) produces the per-flip gap.
3. **MTP comparisons are MTP-aware:** the parity suite for k=4 compares MTP-4 vs MTP-1 (and vs non-MTP as the control) — MTP is NEVER bit-identical to non-MTP at golden level (k=1: 2/12, k=2: 1/12, "depth-stable") because the verify shape inherently changes the ULP (W6); the gate criterion is "no NEW flips beyond the documented near-tie class" (each new flip attributed per rule 2).
4. **Add items to dilute the knife-edge:** suite v1 (40 items) → v2 (≥80 items, the same 0.5 pp bar on the non-knife-edge mass) — with more items, a real regression (moving several items) is caught at the same bar while the single-item knife-edge flips are handled by rule 2.

**Why this is a theory and not just a process note:** without rule 2/3, T1's numerics package CANNOT fully clear the gate — the captured-region drift is irreducible at bf16 (the abotsis "batch-shape numerics (inherent)"; our MTP verify shape is inherently different from non-MTP). The package gets the golden gate MOST of the way (the rowdep and serial-parity sources die; the residual flips are the irreducible class) — and the gate policy is what converts "the residual is documented and bounded" into "adopted". This is the same class of decision as the "tie-aware treatment" the implementation agent recorded after MHC-ROWS; RA-018 makes it explicit and attaches the measurement protocol (per-flip gap attribution) so the policy is enforceable, not a rubber stamp.

**Risks:** the policy could excuse a REAL regression if it hides in the near-tie class — mitigated by (a) the gap must be measured under the treatment config (not asserted), (b) the trap-item list is short and auditable, (c) rule 4 (more items) restores statistical power on the mass. The owner retains the strict gate (today's behavior) by declining rules 2-3 — in which case T1's value is capped at the rowdep/serial-parity wins and the draft graph stays rejected.
